How Mini-Bot recognizes a face

The AI algorithm, the hardware, and the answers to the activity questions.

Additional task checklist

  • Website control and monitoring The Dashboard starts and stops the module, shows the live feed, tunes match strictness and keeps an activity log.
  • Buzzer A buzzer on GPIO 17 beeps three times when a face is not registered or not recognized. The dashboard sends the notification at the same moment.
  • Face display module The Detected face card shows the captured face with a green Recognized or red Not recognized badge.
  • Mini-Bot as the actor The robot on the dashboard smiles for known faces, frowns and shakes for strangers, and sleeps when the system is stopped.

AI algorithm design

Runs once per camera frame

Discussion

Mini-Bot uses face embeddings. The camera frame is shrunk to a quarter of its size so the Raspberry Pi can keep up. A HOG (histogram of oriented gradients) detector finds where faces are. For each face, a small neural network from the face_recognition library (built on dlib) turns the face into a list of 128 numbers that describe its shape.

Recognition is a distance check. The new face's 128 numbers are compared with the saved numbers of every enrolled person. If the closest saved face is within the tolerance (0.5 by default), Mini-Bot names that person. If not, the face is unknown.

An unknown face must stay in view for one second before the buzzer fires. A six-second cooldown stops repeated beeping. These two rules cut false alarms from blurry frames and people walking past.

Lowering the tolerance makes matching stricter: fewer strangers are accepted, but registered people are missed more often. Raising it does the opposite. The slider on the dashboard lets you tune this live.

Activity questions

What are the key components of a face recognition system on a Raspberry Pi, and how do they work together?

Seven parts work in a chain:

  • Raspberry Pi runs everything: capture, recognition, web server and buzzer control.
  • Camera module (CSI port) captures the frames. Its resolution and lens decide how clear a face is.
  • Python and OpenCV read frames, resize them, convert colors and draw the boxes.
  • face_recognition (dlib) detects faces and turns each into a 128-number encoding.
  • Known-faces database holds the enrolled photos and their encodings (the known_faces folder).
  • Flask web app streams the video, shows results and takes commands from the browser.
  • Buzzer on GPIO gives an audible alert for unknown faces.

The camera feeds frames to the Python script. The script detects and encodes faces and compares them with the database. The result goes to the Flask dashboard, and an unknown result also triggers the buzzer.

How does the choice of face recognition algorithm affect accuracy and performance, and which suit a Raspberry Pi?

Better accuracy usually costs more computing time, and a Raspberry Pi has limited power. The choice sets how many frames per second you get and how often faces are missed or confused.

AlgorithmSpeed on a PiAccuracyFit
Haar cascade detection + LBPH recognitionFastLow to medium. Sensitive to light and angle.Pi Zero or Pi 3, simple projects
HOG detection + dlib embeddings (used here)Medium, about 2 to 5 fps on a Pi 4 with a shrunk frameHigh for front-facing facesPi 4 or Pi 5, best balance for this activity
dlib CNN detectionVery slow without a GPUVery high, handles anglesNot suited to a Pi
Lightweight neural nets (YuNet, MobileFaceNet with TensorFlow Lite)FastHighBest upgrade path for real-time use

To speed up any choice: shrink the frame, detect on every second frame, and keep the camera at 640×480.

What are common challenges with face recognition systems, and how can they be addressed?
ChallengeHow to address it
Poor or uneven lightingAdd steady front lighting, avoid backlight, and enroll photos taken in the real location.
Head angle and distanceMount the camera at face height and enroll several photos per person.
Glasses, masks, hatsEnroll the person both with and without the accessory.
False matches and missesTune the tolerance, and require the face to stay in view before alerting.
Photo spoofingAdd liveness checks such as blink detection, or a depth sensor.
Slow processingReduce frame size, skip frames, or use a lighter model.
Privacy and consentEnroll only people who agreed, store data locally and let people be removed.
